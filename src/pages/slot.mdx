  Passkeys and iCloud Keychain autofill need entitlements Apple grants only to browsers, so they cannot work inside any app that hosts a web view - Slot included. Choose another sign-in method, usually your password plus your normal second factor. Slot tells you this before you reach the sign-in page rather than letting it fail silently.

</details>

## Privacy Policy {#privacy-policy}

<p style={{fontStyle: 'italic', color: 'var(--ifm-color-emphasis-600)'}}>Last updated: July 30, 2026</p>

<p>Slot is built by Mateusz Dąbrowski. This policy explains what the Slot app does with your data. The short version: Slot sends nothing to me, and it keeps every account apart from every other one. The longer version matters, because Slot is a container for accounts you already have with Google and Microsoft, and a lot of what happens inside it is between you and them.</p>

    <div style={{marginTop: '2rem'}}>

### What Slot is, and whose policy applies

    </div>

<p>Slot puts each of your Google and Microsoft accounts in its own window, with its own storage. When you use Gmail, Google Calendar, Drive, Meet, Outlook, Teams or any other surface inside Slot, you are using that company's own web application. Their code runs, their servers are contacted, and their privacy policies govern it - exactly as they would in a browser tab.</p>

<ul>
  <li>Google: <a href="https://policies.google.com/privacy">policies.google.com/privacy</a></li>
  <li>Microsoft: <a href="https://privacy.microsoft.com/privacystatement">privacy.microsoft.com/privacystatement</a></li>
</ul>

<p>This policy covers the part that is mine: the app itself.</p>

    <div style={{marginTop: '2rem'}}>

### Nothing reaches me

    </div>

<p>I operate no servers for Slot. There is no account to create, no sign-in to Slot itself, and no way for your data to reach me. Slot contains <strong>no analytics, no telemetry, no crash reporting, no advertising identifiers and no third-party SDKs of any kind</strong> - it is built entirely against Apple's own frameworks and bundles no other company's code.</p>

<p>The only way to send me anything is to choose <strong>Send Feedback</strong> in Settings, which opens a new message to <a href="mailto:slot_feedback@mateuszdabrowski.pl">slot_feedback@mateuszdabrowski.pl</a> in your mail app. You write it, you decide what is in it, and you send it.</p>

    <div style={{marginTop: '2rem'}}>

### Accounts are kept apart

    </div>

<p>Every account gets its own isolated data store. Cookies, sessions and site data from one account are invisible to every other one - this is what lets a work and a personal Google account be signed in at the same time without either being aware of the other, and it is enforced by macOS, not by Slot being careful.</p>

<p>Slot <strong>never asks for, reads, stores or logs your password.</strong> Signing in happens on your provider's own sign-in page, inside a web view, and Slot runs no script on it. Session cookies live inside the account's store, managed by the system; Slot does not read them except to send the Gmail unread request described below, and it never copies them anywhere.</p>

    <div style={{marginTop: '2rem'}}>

### What Slot keeps on your Mac

    </div>

<p>All of this stays on your Mac, in Slot's own container. None of it is uploaded anywhere.</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Your account list</strong> - for each account: a name, any name you set yourself, the address the account reports, the colour you chose, which surfaces you enabled, which browser opens its links, your notification and quiet-hours settings, the calendar feed address if you added one, and the last unread count Slot showed you.</li>
  <li><strong>Profile pictures</strong> - the picture shown on the account rail, cached as a file.</li>
  <li><strong>App settings</strong> - appearance, notification and sound preferences, meeting reminder lead times, and whether Slot asks for Touch ID on unlock.</li>
  <li><strong>Each account's website data</strong> - cookies, local storage and caches, held by macOS in that account's isolated store.</li>
</ul>

<p>Attachments you download go to your Downloads folder. Addresses you copy go to the clipboard. Both are ordinary macOS behaviour and both are yours.</p>

    <div style={{marginTop: '2rem'}}>

### What Slot itself sends, and where

    </div>

<p>Beyond the provider pages you are using, Slot makes exactly four kinds of request of its own:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Your unread count</strong>, from <code>mail.google.com</code>, about once a minute for each signed-in Google account, using that account's own session. The reply contains your address, the unread count, and the sender and subject of the newest unread message.</li>
  <li><strong>Your profile picture</strong>, from <code>googleusercontent.com</code>, once per account. This request carries no cookies.</li>
  <li><strong>Your calendar feed</strong>, but only if you paste an address into Settings - and then only to the address you pasted. Slot accepts <code>https</code> addresses only, sends no cookies and no sign-in, and re-fetches at most every five minutes while it is open. If you have not added a feed, no such request is ever made.</li>
  <li><strong>An attachment you asked to print</strong>, from Gmail's own attachment hosts, only at the moment you press Command-P in Gmail's attachment viewer, using that account's own session. The file goes to the print panel and is then deleted - it is never saved to Downloads and never kept.</li>
</ul>

<p>Two things are worth stating plainly. The calendar feed address is a secret in itself - anyone holding it can read that calendar - which is why Slot refuses to send it unencrypted and refuses an address with a password in it. And when you have chosen to open a document in an external browser, Slot adds your account's address to the link so the right account opens; that link then goes to the browser you picked, and to the provider.</p>

    <div style={{marginTop: '2rem'}}>

### What Slot reads from the pages it shows

    </div>

<p>Slot reads a small number of specific things out of provider pages so it can show them natively. It installs no permanent scripts, registers no channel a page could call into, and bundles no JavaScript of its own. There are four reads, all one-shot and read-only:</p>

<ul>
  <li>The address of your profile picture, from the Gmail page's own header.</li>
  <li>Your unread count and display name, from the Outlook mailbox page, together with the sender and subject of the newest message - the same three things the Gmail feed carries, so a notification can say who wrote.</li>
  <li>Today's meeting times and titles from the Google Meet page - <strong>only</strong> if you switched on the Meet fallback, which exists for accounts whose administrator has disabled calendar feeds.</li>
  <li>The address of the attachment Gmail is showing you, and <strong>only</strong> at the moment you press Command-P while its viewer is open. Slot looks at pictures and frames, never at text, and asks for one address so it can print that file instead of the mailbox behind it.</li>
</ul>

<p>None of them reads a form field, a password, a cookie or a session token, and none reads the body of your mail. Nothing read this way is sent anywhere by Slot.</p>

    <div style={{marginTop: '2rem'}}>

### Notifications

    </div>

<p>When Slot tells you about new mail, the notification carries the account name, the sender, and the subject line - the same preview any mail app shows. Meeting reminders carry the meeting's title. macOS keeps notifications in Notification Center until they are cleared, which is the system's own storage, not Slot's. You can switch mail or meeting notifications off entirely, per account or globally, in Settings.</p>

    <div style={{marginTop: '2rem'}}>

### Camera and microphone

    </div>

<p>Slot requests the camera and microphone only for video calls, and grants them only to Google Meet and Microsoft Teams. Any other page in any account is refused, by Slot, before macOS is asked. You are asked once per account and the answer is remembered; deny it and everything else keeps working.</p>

    <div style={{marginTop: '2rem'}}>

### Deleting your data

    </div>

<p>Removing an account in Slot deletes that account's entire website data store - cookies, sessions, caches - along with its picture and its settings. Nothing is kept for later.</p>

<p>To remove everything at once, delete Slot and then remove <code>~/Library/Containers/mdpl.slot</code>. Signing out is separate: your accounts at Google and Microsoft are unaffected by anything you do in Slot, and are managed with those companies.</p>

    <div style={{marginTop: '2rem'}}>

### Children's privacy

    </div>

<p>Slot is not directed at children. It requires accounts with Google or Microsoft, which have their own age requirements, and it displays whatever those services display.</p>

    <div style={{marginTop: '2rem'}}>

### Changes to this policy

    </div>

<p>If the policy changes, I'll update the "Last updated" date above and post the new version at the same URL. Material changes will be called out in the app's release notes.</p>

    <div style={{marginTop: '2rem'}}>
